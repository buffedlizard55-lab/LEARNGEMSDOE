The 19-band feature stack

One multiband GeoTIFF, 100 m, UTM 11N. This page is the band-level inventory: what each band is officially called, which geophysical family it belongs to, where the underlying survey came from, and — importantly — which publicly available layers are not in the stack.

Provenance caveat, stated up front. The band names and data categories below are the description and data_category tags read out of training_features.tif by the official reference-solution notebook. The competition pages do not state which public release each band was derived from, what processing was applied, at what native resolution it was acquired, or how nodata was propagated. Anything beyond the band tag on this page is labelled as an inference.

Only two of nineteen bands are topographic, and both are derived from a 100 m detrended surface. That is the single clearest structural reason to bring in outside elevation data (see H3 and the geomorphology domain).

Derived observation: the prediction grid is larger than the GeoDAWN survey area

Arithmetic from two verified numbers: the raster is 3292 × 3730 pixels at 100 m, i.e. 329.2 km × 373.0 km ≈ 122,800 km² of bounding box. The GeoDAWN survey covers 51,857 km². The bounding box is therefore roughly 2.4× the surveyed area.

Confidence: inference. Both inputs are verified; the conclusion is arithmetic, but neither the competition pages nor the rules state how much of the prediction grid lies inside the GeoDAWN footprint, and no one has measured it here because the rasters are not placed. If it holds, the implication is that a substantial part of the scoring grid is served by regional, lower-resolution geophysics rather than by GeoDAWN — and any method tuned on GeoDAWN-density lineaments would behave differently there. Worth checking first once data are in data/: overlay the GeoDAWN survey-outline shapefile on the raster bounds.

Known processing pitfalls

  • Nodata sentinel. Features use −3.4028234663852886e+38; the reference notebook masks anything below −1e38 to NaN before normalising. Any local preprocessing must do the same or the min-max normalisation will be destroyed.
  • Per-band min-max normalisation is what the reference solution applies (across the whole raster, over non-NaN values). It is sensitive to outliers — worth re-checking per band before trusting it.
  • The label raster is int32 with nodata −1.0, so “not a fault” and “no data” are different values. Treat −1 as nodata, not as background.
  • Resampled provenance is undocumented. Several bands could be resampled from grids of very different native resolution. Any gradient you compute on top of an already-resampled grid inherits that smoothing; the GeoDAWN line spacing above is the lower bound on resolvable wavelength in the magnetic bands.
  • Two seismic bands share parameters (n=100 km, a=15°), so bands 10 and 16 are not independent evidence.

Reproduce the inventory locally

bash scripts/download_competition_data.sh   # on an enrolled machine, into data/
python scripts/prepare_data.py              # writes data/inventory.json (inspection only)

prepare_data.py dumps CRS, resolution, size, band count, dtypes, nodata and per-band tags for every GeoTIFF it finds. Run it first after data placement and compare its output with the table above; if they disagree, the table on this page is wrong and should be corrected here.
