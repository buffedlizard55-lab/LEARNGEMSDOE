Find the faults the catalogue missed

The GEMS Prize is not scored on how well you reproduce the published fault map — the published map is masked out of the scoring. It is scored on faults that experts have identified and that are absent from the USGS / INGENIOUS catalogue in the GeoDAWN area. This site is the research library and hypothesis backlog behind that problem: every entry is tied to a primary source you can open and check.

What is actually being scored

The private test set for Phase 1 was labelled by experts before the competition and is fixed. In Phase 2 an expert panel reviews every team's submission, verifies anything genuinely new, and all submissions are re-scored against the expanded set — so a prediction that helps experts find a real unmapped fault can score higher in Phase 2 than in Phase 1 (competition structure).

Two clarifications from DrivenData staff change the optimal strategy materially, and both are quoted verbatim on the governance page:

  1. Existing catalogue pixels are masked from evaluation in both rounds — the mask is pixel-exact, not buffered.
  2. A new-fault ground-truth pixel may lie within 300 m of a known trace; staff describe finding those corrections as “one outcome we are aiming for”.

Net effect: reproducing the catalogue earns nothing, and hedging a wide corridor around known traces costs real false-positive mass. The money is in geometry an expert would add — tip extensions, splays, parallel strands, and structures under cover — not in thicker ink on mapped lines.

Flagged irregularities

These are recorded, not smoothed over. Each one is a place where the official documents disagree with each other, or where a question put to the organisers has no answer.

  1. Deadline wording differs between documents. The hub states “Dec. 3, 2026, 11:59 p.m. UTC”; rules §A.1 states “5:00 p.m. ET on the prize submission deadline date”. Both are official. Treat the earlier instant as binding and submit well before it. (hub · §A.1)
  2. Eligibility text is shorter on the homepage than in the rules. The hub says only that a team captain must be a US citizen or permanent resident; §1.3 adds that other team members must be “legally authorized to work in the United States”, excludes FFRDC-affiliated researchers from cash prizes, and excludes participants in Malign Foreign Talent Recruitment Programs. Two forum threads asking which text governs are unanswered (see governance).
  3. Test-fault provenance will not be published. Staff: “We're not sharing details about the data sources, fault types, or coverage behind the test faults beyond what's in the problem description.” (11527 post 7)
  4. The reference notebook prints the label raster as “Band 19”. Staff confirm this is a bug in the summary string; the label file has one band. (11529)
  5. Feature-stack provenance is not documented per band. The 19-band raster carries band descriptions and data categories, but the competition pages do not state which public release each band was derived from, at what resolution it was resampled, or how nodata was handled. Any provenance claim beyond the band description is an inference and is labelled as such.
  6. No training pipeline exists in this repository. A prior handoff stated the train→inference→validate pipeline was “ready to run”; it is not present in scripts/. See pipeline status. Re-checked this session: still true. This agent did not add one.
  7. Rules PDF host redirect. The official URL nlr.gov/docs/fy26osti/96647.pdf redirected to docs.nlr.gov when fetched on 2026-09-26. Keep the NLR URL as the citation. The change-log table in the PDF preface was still empty.
  8. Staff about-post links to page 966. The pinned forum welcome links to page 966, which is a duplicate of the hub welcome, not the problem description (page 967).
  9. Public downloads failed TLS from this machine; the files were not treated as missing. curl to usgs.gov, sciencebase.gov, gdr.openei.org and web2.nbmg.unr.edu returned SSL_ERROR_SYSCALL. HTML and JSON were still readable through a separate fetch path. The ScienceBase catalog lists the outline zips. Do not conclude they do not exist.
  10. Catalogue attribute conventions are mixed. On the NBMG service, MAPSCALE stores both short codes and the literal string 1:10,000, and REC2023 stores both <15,000-style bins and bare numbers such as 8400. Do not normalize them until the field-definition text is read. Counts: requirements page.
  11. Two instructions for how much area the GeoTIFF must cover. Rules §3.2 says “for the entirety of the GeoDAWN study area”; the problem description says “the same bounds as the training data”. The grid bounding box is roughly 2.4× the surveyed area, so these are not obviously the same instruction. Unresolvable until the raster is placed — GV-12.
  12. Official documents disagree about whether GeoDAWN includes lidar. Rules §2 calls it “a high-resolution lidar, magnetic, and radiometric study”; the data release is titled “Airborne magnetic and radiometric surveys” and describes the 3DEP lidar as a coordinated but separate collection. No scoring consequence is known — GM-9 / GV-13.
