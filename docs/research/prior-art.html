PA-1 · DrivenData reference solution (Lipor) — design choices

verified confidence: high

github.com/drivendataorg/gems-prize-reference-solution · notebook unet-mc-cv-reference-solution.ipynb · author page linked in README: John Lipor, PSU

Documented design:

  • U-Net ensemble with Monte Carlo cross-validation (random train/test splits).
  • Inputs: multi-channel 100 m geospatial features (19 bands in the printed run).
  • Patches, data augmentation, Tversky loss weighted to penalize false negatives more than false positives (segmentation_models_pytorch.losses.TverskyLoss).
  • Average predictions from the best model across iterations.
  • Device: CUDA, else MPS, else CPU. Training described as much faster on GPU.
  • Expects local files data/numeric_features.tif and data/labels.tif.
  • Printed raster: 3292 × 3730, EPSG:32611, 100 m, features float32, labels int32, label nodata −1.

Staff erratum: the notebook summary string that prints the label band as “Band 19” is an artifact; the label GeoTIFF has a single band (forum).

PA-2 · Where that reference is likely to fall short of the scored target

reasoning from verified facts — not an empirical score

  1. Trains on the catalogue that scoring masks out. Labels are existing USGS/INGENIOUS faults (rules §3.3). Staff: those pixels are excluded from evaluation. A network that reproduces the training raster maximizes the wrong objective unless it also generalizes to unmapped geometry.
  2. No catalogue-gap sampling. Nothing in the README/notebook describes masking known traces, sampling negatives near traces, or targeting along-strike extensions.
  3. 100 m only. The notebook path does not ingest the 1 m DEM listed in the problem description.
  4. Random patch splits vs spatial holdout. Monte Carlo random splits on overlapping patches leak spatial autocorrelation; they do not simulate “new faults in unmapped corridors.”
  5. Tversky on catalogue pixels ≠ DTI on new faults. Loss alignment with α=0.2, β=0.8 is directionally right, but the 300 m distance kernel and the known-fault mask are not in the training loop (not present in the overview cells fetched).

These are design-gap arguments. This repo has not benchmarked the reference notebook (no data, no GPU run).

PA-3 · Hermant et al., 2025 — CNN on DEM + Sentinel-2 in Nevada

verified confidence: high

PDF · listed on About

Claims: CNN trained on 128×128×3 images (elevation, slope, Sentinel-2 B8A) with human-mapped labels on one half of a north-central Nevada area; prediction on the other half. Rejected USGS QFFD as insufficiently accurate/homogeneous for training. 1,100 faults / 264 km of labels; 50 m raster buffer. Lidar-derived 3DEP only. Authors argue regional QFFD density differences are partly mapping effort, not only geology.

Verified 2026-09-26 against the PDF: the geographic split is explicit — "a new manual mapping of faults over the eastern half of the study area … the remaining half was reserved for prediction" — alongside a random 64/16/20 tile split within the labeled half; the DEM is 1/3-arc-second (~10 m) 3DEP derived from lidar (chosen over 1 m "because it requires less computational space"); the B8A band was chosen for vegetation contrast; training uses Focal Binary Cross-Entropy; metrics are PR-AUC and weighted-Focal-IoU.

Relevance: Closest published analog in the same province. Lessons: (1) do not treat QFFD as perfect labels; (2) optical/vegetation can mark traces that DEM slope misses; (3) they still map surface faults — buried geophysical structures are out of scope for that CNN.

Limitation they share with the reference U-Net: supervised on mapped traces, so they predict “what mappers already know how to draw,” unless the train region is spatially disjoint from the test region (they did use a geographic split).
