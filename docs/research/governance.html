GV-1 · Eligibility: the homepage is shorter than the rules

Homepage says
“Individual competitors must be U.S. citizens or permanent residents. Teams must have a captain who is a U.S. citizen or permanent resident. Private entities must be incorporated in and maintain a primary place of business in the United States. Federal entities and federal employees are not eligible to participate.”
Rules §1.3 adds
  • “Individuals competing as part of a team are eligible to participate if they are legally authorized to work in the United States.”
  • “Academic institutions must be based in the United States and accredited by a nationally recognized accrediting agency or association.”
  • FFRDCs may not compete; individual FFRDC researchers may compete in their individual capacities if no FFRDC resources are used, but “may receive an honorable mention [and] are not eligible to receive any cash prizes”.
  • Non-DOE Federal entities and Federal employees are not eligible; nor are DrivenData officers, employees, judges and their households; DOE employees and support contractors; debarred parties; under-18s; individuals in a Malign Foreign Talent Recruitment Program of a foreign country of concern; and entities owned or controlled by such a government. DOE defines foreign countries of concern to include Iran, North Korea, Russia, Belarus and China, “subject to change”.
  • Registration requires certifying under penalty of perjury, with exposure under 18 U.S.C. §1001 and §287 and 31 U.S.C. §§3729–3733 and 3801–3812.
Open questions
Two forum threads ask which text governs and both are unanswered at 2026-09-26: 11540 (must non-captain members be US-work-authorised?) and 11526 (is the one-final-submission limit applied per team or per organisation / per UEI?). A third, older question in the category thread — 11499 — asks whether a team of one US resident and one non-US resident may participate, and is also unanswered.
Action
Re-confirm eligibility against §1.3 before any change in team composition or affiliation. Until 11540 is answered, the conservative reading is that §1.3 governs: every team member needs US work authorisation and the captain needs citizenship or permanent residency.

GV-2 · Generative AI must be disclosed in the narrative (§3.2)

“Using generative AI technology in the development of your prize submission is allowed. However, you must indicate in the narrative (not included in the word count) the extent to which, if any, you used generative AI technology and how you used it to develop your submission (including all submission elements described in this official rules document). You are responsible for the accuracy, authenticity, and authorship representations of your submission under consideration, including content developed with generative AI tools. Relying on generative AI may introduce significant risks, including but not limited to, research misconduct resulting from fabrication, falsification, or plagiarism when proposing, performing, or reviewing research or in reporting research results.” Official rules §3.2, Process Overview

That is the compliance basis for the AI-usage log, which is maintained incrementally, session by session, rather than reconstructed at the deadline. The log records the tool used, what was done, what was deliberately not done, and which claims still need human verification.

Related, from §3.2: prize finalists must submit “their solution's complete code assets and documentation”, including a description of the resources required to build and run the solution, sufficient to reproduce the winning results, consistent with DrivenData's Winning Model Documentation Template.

GV-3 · Submission mechanics

  • Three scored submissions per week (§3.2, §3.4), resetting on a rolling window, not a calendar week (forum 11524).
  • One final submission for both rounds. “By the submission deadline, you must select only one set of predictions to use as your final submission” (§3.4). “Multiple finalized submissions are not allowed” and “individuals participating on a team will not be allowed to submit a separate final submission”.
  • You choose blind. “You must make your decision without knowledge of your scores on the private test set. This restriction is in place to encourage models that generalize well to unseen data and to discourage overfitting to the public test set” (§3.6.2).
  • Public leaderboard scores may not match final scores (§3.2) — the public test set is a subset of the new-fault labels, and the relative weighting of faults in the two test sets is set by the organisers (§3.6.2).
  • Winners are notified approximately 60 days after the prize closes (§3.6.5); DOE may hold short, optional interviews with winners (§3.6.3); a DOE Federal employee is designated judge and makes the final determination (§3.6.4, A.15).

GV-4 · External data: licence plus shareability

“Participants are allowed to use any additional data sources, provided that the participants possess a license that permits the data to be used in this challenge and shared with the sponsor for evaluation purposes.” Problem description — External datasets; reaffirmed by staff in forum 11528

Staff's answer to a direct question about subscription-only data was to point back at that sentence, which reads as: the licence must permit the sponsor to receive it, not merely permit you to hold it. Practically, this favours public-domain and CC-licensed sources. Both major sources used in this research qualify — GeoDAWN is CC0 1.0 and the INGENIOUS compilation is CC BY 4.0 (redistributable with attribution).

GV-5 · The scoring mask is pixel-exact

Asked directly whether the mask is pixel-exact or buffered, and whether a new-fault ground-truth pixel may sit near a known trace, staff answered on 2026-09-21:

  1. “The mask is indeed pixel-exact — it is identical to the provided set of training fault labels.”
  2. “Only new-fault ground truth is considered for scoring purposes. A predicted pixel that is near a known fault trace but far from a new-fault ground truth pixel will be fully penalized, i.e., the buffer does not apply to known faults.”
  3. “A new-fault ground truth pixel can indeed lie within 300m of a known fault trace. Such pixels would constitute corrections or modifications to existing fault traces. Identifying these corrections is one outcome we are aiming for as part of this competition. Such corrections may already exist in the new-fault set, and may also exist in the final round evaluation set.”

Earlier in the same thread staff had already confirmed that known-fault pixels are masked from evaluation in both rounds and that “for scoring purposes it should not matter whether these known faults are included with predictions or not” (post 2).

Strategic reading. Point 2 is the one that costs people: hedging a few pixels either side of a mapped trace is not free, it is full false-positive mass. Point 3 is the one that pays: corrections near known traces are an explicitly intended outcome. Together they say — be precise where the catalogue is precise, and be bold where it is wrong.

GV-6 · What counts as a new fault

“For the purposes of this competition, ‘new fault’ means ‘any fault pixel not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS’ and can include newly mapped geometry of an existing fault system.” Staff reply, forum 11536

Asked whether a continuation beyond a mapped endpoint, or a newly mapped splay or parallel strand within the same fault zone, counts — the answer covers all of it. This is the official grounding for hypothesis H1, and it is the single most useful clarification in the forum for deciding what to predict.

GV-7 · Test-fault provenance will not be published

“We're not sharing details about the data sources, fault types, or coverage behind the test faults beyond what's in the problem description. Note that the largest prize pool (Phase 2) will use a test set that is updated by expert review of all Phase 1 submissions, so your fault predictions have an impact on final evaluation even if they are not the most performant in Phase 1.” Staff reply, forum 11527, post 7

The question asked three things — which data the NLR and USGS experts used, whether the new faults are surface features or buried/inferred structures, and whether the whole region was reviewed at uniform detail. All three were declined. Constraints on strategy that follow: do not build a single-modality bet; make the Phase 2 narrative strong, because that is where staff have pointed; and do not expect to validate a buried-fault detector against Phase 1 labels that may contain none.

Separately, rules §2 does state who labelled them: “a set of newly identified faults labeled by geology experts at the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) and USGS.”

GV-9 · Other provisions worth knowing before you write anything down

  • Public elements are public. §A.4: elements designated public “must not include trade secrets or business-sensitive, proprietary, or otherwise confidential information”, and teams grant DOE an unlimited licence to display and use them. Confidential material must be marked with the prescribed notice on every page and with double brackets around every line.
  • Submission materials become DOE records subject to FOIA (§A.10); DOE will notify competitors of FOIA requests under 29 C.F.R. §70.26.
  • Payment mechanics. §A.2: winners must return an NLR ACH form and an IRS W-9 within 30 days of notice; failure can mean disqualification and no funds. §A.3: a single amount is awarded to the designated primary submitter, who is solely responsible for allocating it among teammates — DOE will not arbitrate disputes.
  • Program policy factors (§A.13) sit alongside reviewer scores in the judge's decision: advancing DOE and administration policy priorities; geographic diversity and economic impact; non-duplication with existing DOE funding; technological or programme diversity versus the existing portfolio; likely US employment and manufacturing benefit; acceleration of advances industry would not undertake alone; support for complementary DOE-funded efforts; expanding DOE funding to new recipients; enabling new market segments.
  • Risk review (§A.12). “DOE may conduct a risk review, using government resources, of the competitor and project personnel for potential risks of foreign interference. … An elimination based on a risk review is not appealable.”
  • DOE may award nothing. §A.12: “If, in DOE's determination, no competitors are likely to achieve the goals of the program, DOE will select no competitors to be winners and will award no prize money.”
  • Prize amounts. §1.1: Phase 1 $50,000 split equally among the top five; Phase 2 $100,000 / $70,000 / $40,000 / $25,000 / $15,000. “Up to 10 awards” across both phases.
  • Teams are encouraged to be multidisciplinary. §2: “competitors are strongly encouraged to form multidisciplinary teams that include expertise in data science and geosciences, such as geophysics or structural geology.”

GV-17 · The problem description's feature list and the notebook's 19 band tags do not line up

GV-17 Two named layers have no band tag; two band tags are absent from the description

verified for both texts · the consequences are inference

Sources
Problem description, provided features (read 2026-09-26); the band tags as printed by the reference-solution notebook (see the feature stack).
Problem description, verbatim
The GeoTIFF has many layers: Surface conductivity and depth to conductive base surface · Detrended elevation and the slope of detrended elevation · Dilatation rate, shear strain rate, and the second invariant of the strain rate tensor · Isostatic gravity anomaly and the slope of the isostatic gravity anomaly · Magnetics including reduced-to-pole magnetic anomaly, total magnetic intensity, the vertical and horizontal slope of total magnetic intensity, and the top-of-crustal magnetic source depth estimate · Density of earthquakes.Problem description, provided features
Band tags, verbatim examples
band 6 “Tilt angle or total curvature — magnetic field derivative for edge detection”; band 10 “Distance to earthquake (n=100km radius, a=15° azimuth parameters)”; band 15 “Depth to basement surface — thickness of sedimentary cover”.
The mismatch, stated precisely

The problem description names a “depth to conductive base surface” and a “top-of-crustal magnetic source depth estimate”; no band tag matches either name. Conversely, band 6 (tilt angle / total curvature) and band 10 (distance to earthquake) have no counterpart in the problem description's list. The remaining items map onto bands plausibly but not provably (e.g. “surface conductivity” ↔ band 17).

Why it is not pedantry
Band 6 is already an edge detector, so whether it exists changes what H2 must build; the two unnamed depth surfaces would change the cover-conditioning argument (PF-6); and if the problem description's list is the authoritative inventory, the notebook's tags are a display convention and the band numbering used across this site could be shifted by the position of the unlisted layers.
Action
Resolve by comparing data/inventory.json (written by prepare_data.py) against the feature-stack page once the rasters are placed. Until then, every band number on this site is cited as “the tag printed by the reference notebook”, which is what the feature-stack page states up front.

GV-12 · Open wording difference: how much area must the GeoTIFF cover?

GV-12 "The entirety of the GeoDAWN study area" vs "the same bounds as the training data"

Sources
Rules §3.2 PDF; problem description submission format.
Claim (both verbatim)
You must submit a single GeoTIFF with a single raster layer at 100-meter resolution containing your model's predictions of fault locations for the entirety of the GeoDAWN study area.Official Rules, §3.2
Your submission has the same bounds as the training data, and data outside the bounds is null or nan.Problem description, Submission format
Why it is a live question, not pedantry
The prediction grid (3292 × 3730 px at 100 m ≈ 122,800 km² of bounding box) is roughly 2.4× the surveyed area (51,857 km² — feature stack). If the training raster's bounds are that bounding box, then these are two different instructions, and the second is the larger one. If the training raster is clipped to the footprint, they coincide. It cannot be settled without the file.
