Potential-field geophysics

Buried structural contrasts in the GeoDAWN / Great Basin stack. Methods below are cited to primary papers or official data releases. No derivative grids were computed this session (no local rasters).

PF-1 · GeoDAWN airborne magnetic and radiometric surveys

verified confidence: high

Source: Glen, J.M.G., and Earney, T.E., 2024, GeoDAWN: Airborne magnetic and radiometric surveys of the northwestern Great Basin, Nevada and California: USGS data release, 10.5066/P93LGLVQ · ScienceBase item 657e1d85d34e23d3533209f7

Claims: USGS and DOE acquired high-resolution aeromagnetic and aeroradiometric data over northern/western Nevada and eastern California to support geologic and geophysical mapping for geothermal and critical-mineral studies (Walker Lane and western Great Basin). EarthMRI + DOE GTO. Contractor EDCON-PRJ, 1 Nov 2021 – 20 Nov 2022. Two overlapping surveys: Area 1 (Clayton Valley; rank-1 EarthMRI specs; 200 m lines, 2000 m ties, ~100–150 m terrain clearance) and Area 2 (remainder; 400 m lines, 4000 m ties, ~150–200 m clearance). Combined 149,030 line-km, 51,857 km², four blocks (Winnemucca, Fallon, Hawthorne, Tonopah). Magnetic processing: diurnal, aircraft field, tie-line and micro-leveling, IGRF. In steep terrain, actual clearance can deviate from the planned drape; “variable terrain clearance should be considered when modeling and interpreting these data.” Coordinated 3DEP lidar over a similar extent.

Relevance: This is the named feature-data citation in the prize rules (footnote 3). Line-spacing difference (200 m vs 400 m) implies spatially variable resolution of magnetic lineaments — a possible false-positive/false-negative pattern if models treat the mosaic as uniform. Terrain-clearance variation can mimic or mask shallow magnetic edges.

Confidence: High. Flight-spec numbers are from the ScienceBase summary, not reconstructed.

PF-2 · What the competition feature GeoTIFF actually contains (magnetics/gravity)

verified confidence: medium–high

Sources: Problem description — provided features; reference notebook band printout in unet-mc-cv-reference-solution.ipynb.

Problem description list: isostatic gravity anomaly and its slope; RTP magnetic anomaly; TMI; vertical and horizontal slope of TMI; top-of-crustal magnetic source depth estimate.

Notebook tag printout (19 bands, 3730×3292, EPSG:32611, 100 m) includes, among others: magnetic anomaly; reduced-to-pole; TMI horizontal gradient; “tilt angle or total curvature” (tag text); TMI vertical gradient; TMI; isostatic gravity and its slope, vertical gradient, and horizontal gradient; depth to basement surface.

Relevance: Gradient and tilt-style layers are already in the 100 m stack, so a model can use them without recomputing from line data — but at 100 m, not at flight-line resolution. Depth-to-basement / magnetic-source depth is a cover-thickness proxy for “younger cover masking scarps.”

Confidence: High that those layers exist in the reference run. Medium that DrivenData’s download filename is training_features.tif (problem description) vs numeric_features.tif (notebook) — both names are documented; they may be the same file renamed. Re-check after local download.

PF-3 · Tilt derivative (Miller & Singh, 1994)

verified confidence: high on the citation; medium on GeoDAWN-specific performance

Citation: Miller, H.G., and Singh, V., 1994, Potential field tilt—a new concept for location of potential field sources: Journal of Applied Geophysics, v. 32, p. 213–217. (Cited as the first tilt-angle filter in later literature, e.g. reviews of balanced edge filters.)

Claim (standard definition, as restated in later papers that quote the formula): TDR = atan( (∂F/∂z) / |∇h F| ), which equalizes amplitude of shallow and deep sources and highlights edges. This session did not fetch the paywalled 1994 PDF itself.

Relevance: The reference notebook’s band 6 tag reads “Tilt angle or total curvature - magnetic field derivative for edge detection.” If that tag is accurate, Miller–Singh-type information is already a feature. Tilt zeros/edges are candidate buried contacts under basins where QFFD has no surface trace.

Confidence: Citation year/title/journal/pages corroborated by multiple secondary citations. Full 1994 PDF not opened here — marked as such. Do not treat a specific TDR threshold as fact.

PF-4 · Total horizontal derivative of tilt (Verduzco et al., 2004)

verified as a published method confidence: medium (PDF not opened)

Citation: Verduzco, B., Fairhead, J.D., Green, C.M., and MacKenzie, C., 2004, New insights into magnetic derivatives for structural mapping: The Leading Edge, v. 23, p. 116–119.

Claim (as restated in later papers): the total horizontal derivative of the tilt angle (THDR) is an improved edge detector relative to tilt alone.

Relevance: Candidate extra feature if not already identical to the provided “tilt angle or total curvature” band. Computing THDR from 100 m grids is cheap; computing it from GeoDAWN line data is not yet done.

Confidence: Bibliographic identity is consistent across independent citations. The 2004 TLE PDF was not fetched this session.

PF-5 · Regional Great Basin gravity/magnetic maps used by INGENIOUS

verified confidence: high

Citation: Glen, J.M.G., Earney, T.E., Zielinski, L.A., Schermerhorn, W.D., Dean, B.J., and Hardwick, C., 2022, Regional geophysical maps of the Great Basin, USA: USGS data release, 10.5066/P9Z6SA1Z

Claims: Provisional regional gravity grid from USGS/UGS, industry, Ponce (1997) Nevada compilation, and Hildenbrand et al. (2002) public-domain gravity; regional magnetic grid from Bankey et al. (2002) North American compilation; depth-to-basement from Shah and Boyd (2018). Compiled to support geothermal characterization and INGENIOUS (DE-EE0009254).

Relevance: Distinguishes regional potential-field compilations (INGENIOUS inputs) from GeoDAWN high-resolution magnetics. Catalogue-gap models should not confuse the two resolutions. Buried basin-bounding structures often show first in gravity; GeoDAWN magnetics may refine them inside the survey outline.

Confidence: High.
