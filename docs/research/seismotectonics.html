Strain-rate invariants, earthquake density, conductivity

Seismotectonics and strain

ST-1 · Layers in the official 100 m stack

verified confidence: high

Problem description · notebook band list

Problem description: dilatation rate, shear strain rate, and the second invariant of the strain rate tensor; density of earthquakes; surface conductivity and depth to conductive base surface.

Notebook tags: geodetic second invariant; geodetic shear rate; geodetic dilatation rate; “Distance to earthquake (n=100km radius, a=15° azimuth parameters)”; “Earthquake intensity or density (n=100km radius, a=15° parameters)”; conductivity surface.

Relevance: Independent kinematic and electrical evidence. Agreement among strain, seismicity, and a potential-field edge off the catalogue is a classic hidden-structure argument. Disagreement (high strain, no seismicity, no gravity edge) is a caution against over-predicting.

Confidence: High that these bands exist. The “n=100km, a=15°” parameterization is from the notebook’s GeoTIFF tags, not from the problem-description prose — re-read tags after download.

ST-2 · INGENIOUS geodetic and seismicity products

verified confidence: high on existence

Ayling et al., 2022, 10.15121/1881483 · GDR resource list

Claims: Compilation includes geotiffs of geodetic shear and dilation models (Nevada Geodetic Laboratory) and independent/dependent earthquake density (Nevada). Project publications list Kreemer, C., and Young, Z.M., 2022, Crustal strain rates in the Western U.S. and their relationship with earthquake rates: Seismological Research Letters, 10.1785/0220220153.

Relevance: Likely provenance of the competition strain and seismicity bands (not independently confirmed by DrivenData metadata in this session). Kreemer & Young relate strain rates to earthquake rates — useful for “where independent signals agree.”

Confidence: High that the GDR zips exist (geodetics zip, seismicity zip). The SRL article abstract was not fully extracted (publisher page). No numerical strain values are quoted here.

ST-3 · Electrical conductance (MT) of the Great Basin

verified confidence: high

Peacock, J.R., and Bedrosian, P., 2022, Electrical Conductance Maps of the Great Basin, USA: USGS data release, 10.5066/P9TWT2LU (linked from INGENIOUS GDR and GBCGE project page).

GDR blurb: electrical conductance maps from a 3D model of the Great Basin at five depth ranges spanning 2 to 200 km.

Relevance: Conductive zones can mark fluids and clay along faults. A conductivity anomaly without a catalogue trace is a candidate gap; a conductivity anomaly on a mapped range-front fault is not. Depth-range mismatch vs 100 m surface labels is a real limitation.

Confidence: High on the data release. The competition “surface conductivity and depth to conductive base” layers may be derived from this or from a different product — unverified which.

ST-4 · Slip and dilation tendency on Quaternary faults

verified

Siler, D.L., 2022, Shapefile for slip tendency and dilation tendency calculated for Quaternary faults in the Great Basin: 10.5066/P9YL58W6

Claim (GDR): slip- and dilation-tendency analysis of Quaternary faults in the Great Basin region.

Relevance: Attributes of already mapped faults, useful for geothermal favorability, weaker as a detector of unmapped traces. Could still rank which catalogue gaps (if found) matter for Phase 2 geologic narrative.
