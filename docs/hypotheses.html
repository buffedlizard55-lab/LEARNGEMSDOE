H1 — Newly mapped geometry of existing systems

untested

Layers: training-label raster (mask); 100 m detrended elevation / slope; 1 m DEM if placed; RTP/TMI horizontal gradient.

Physical signature: A slope break or magnetic/gravity edge that continues past the digitized endpoint of a QFFD/INGENIOUS trace, or a parallel strand / splay within a few hundred metres of the mapped line.

Why a catalogue gap, not a known fault: Staff defined “new fault” as any pixel not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS, explicitly including new geometry of an existing system (11536). Known pixels are masked from scoring (staff 11516), so those extra corridor pixels are not free — they cost FP unless they match new-fault labels — but staff also said new-fault pixels may lie within 300 m of known traces and that finding those corrections is an intended outcome (11516). Hermant et al. report local QFFD misfit up to 400 m in north-central Nevada, which is larger than the 300 m kernel.

Expected DTI impact: High if private labels include tip extensions (β=0.8 punishes missing them). High FP cost if we thicken every range front indiscriminately (mask does not buffer).

Cost: Low–medium. Morphological extrapolation along strike plus a narrow corridor model; no new data purchase.

Status: Untested. Reject if a spatial holdout shows the extra corridor is mostly cultural/irrigation linears or lithologic contacts.

H2 — Buried intra-basin structures (potential-field edges under cover)

untested

Layers: isostatic gravity and its gradients; RTP / TMI gradients / tilt-style band; depth-to-basement / magnetic-source depth; not the training-label raster as a positive class.

Physical signature: Linear gravity or magnetic gradient maxima (or tilt-derivative edges) beneath basins where depth-to-basement is large and DEM slope is low — density/susceptibility contrast without a surface scarp.

Why a catalogue gap: USGS QFFD is built on geologic evidence of coseismic surface deformation (USGS faults page). Buried structures without a published surface-rupture study are out of that design. The About page states many GeoDAWN-region faults are hidden and require geophysics (page 968). Staff will not say whether private test labels include those structures (11527/7).

Expected DTI impact: High if Phase 1 private labels include geophysical faults; harmful (pure FP) if those labels are lidar scarps only. Phase 2 expert review can still credit them even if Phase 1 DTI is weaker (same staff post). Do not spend a weekly slot on an H2-only map.

Cost: Low on 100 m grids; high if reprocessing GeoDAWN line data.

Status: Untested. Protocol will not be published (CG-5); keep as a Phase 2-oriented component, not the whole submission.

H3 — 1 m lidar scarps absent from the compiled map

untested

Layers: 1 m DEM from 1m_DEM_links.csv (not yet downloaded); 100 m detrended elevation as a coarse prior; catalogue mask.

Physical signature: Linear breaks-in-slope, 1–10 m high, persistent along strike, on piedmonts or playa margins; may be invisible at 100 m. Hermant: lidar-derived slope is the usable scarp layer; non-lidar 3DEP slope is artefact-ridden.

Why a catalogue gap: OF 93-338 compilations prefer existing topical literature and mixed map scales. A scarp that became mappable only after 3DEP/GeoDAWN lidar is exactly the class Hermant remapped by hand. QFFD geomorphic-expression examples include 1–2 m scarps — below 100 m resolution.

Expected DTI impact: High if experts used lidar (likely, not verified). Risk: drainage ditches, shorelines, lithologic benches.

Cost: High I/O and compute (tile download, curvature/slope at 1 m, then aggregate to 100 m probability).

Status: Untested. Blocked on DEM placement.

H4 — Kinematic orphans (strain / seismicity without a catalogue trace)

untested

Layers: dilatation, shear, second invariant; earthquake density / distance-to-earthquake bands; catalogue mask.

Physical signature: High strain-rate invariant and/or earthquake density whose nearest catalogue pixel is >300 m away.

Why a catalogue gap: Geodetic strain and seismicity do not require a published scarp. INGENIOUS compiled these as independent evidence layers (GDR). A kinematic orphan is a place the surface catalogue is silent while the crust is deforming.

Expected DTI impact: Diffuse highs will spray FP (α=0.2 still not free). Use as a spatial prior to up-weight H1–H3, not as a standalone probability map.

Cost: Low (already in the 100 m stack).

Status: Untested. First measurement after prepare_data.py: quantile overlay vs distance-to-label.

H5 — Do not optimize the reference U-Net on catalogue pixels

untested

Layers: all 19 feature bands; labels used only as a mask and hard-negative near-trace sampler, not as the sole positive class.

Physical signature: n/a — this is a training-design hypothesis.

Why a catalogue gap: The reference solution trains a U-Net to reproduce USGS/INGENIOUS traces (PA-1). Those traces are masked at score time (GV-5). The model can only help if it generalizes off the ink. Proposed alternative: positives = morphological extensions + geophysical edges + lidar scarps that are off the mask; negatives = random and near-trace unmasked pixels.

Expected DTI impact: Necessary condition for any DL approach to beat “copy the catalogue.” Unknown magnitude.

Cost: Medium (retraining). GPU required for a serious run (reference README).

Status: Untested. This agent will not run training or write a submission GeoTIFF.

H6 — Conductivity as corroboration, not a detector

untested

Layers: surface conductivity, depth to conductive base (competition stack); Peacock & Bedrosian 2022 MT conductance maps if licensed for sharing.

Physical signature: Conductive lineament or shallow conductive base coincident with an H1–H4 candidate.

Why a catalogue gap: Fluids/clays along an unmapped structure can raise conductance without a QFFD entry. Using MT alone will light up basins and geothermal outflow, which are not faults.

Expected DTI impact: Small, as a multiplier. Negative result expected if used as a primary map.

Cost: Low on provided bands.

Status: Untested. Provenance of the competition conductivity band vs USGS MT release is unverified (ST-3).

H7 — Control: GeoDAWN flight-spec mosaic

untested

Layers: magnetic gradients; GeoDAWN Area 1 vs Area 2 outline (200 m vs 400 m lines — Glen & Earney 2024).

Physical signature: Apparent lineament density jump at the Area 1/Area 2 boundary that is not present in gravity, DEM, or catalogue.

Why this is a control, not a gap: If magnetic “faults” follow the survey mosaic, they are acquisition artifacts. Reject H2 locally where that happens.

Expected DTI impact: Prevents FP. No TP expected.

Cost: Low once survey shapefiles from ScienceBase are downloaded (public).

Status: Untested. Public shapefiles not yet pulled (out of scope for login-walled competition data, but allowed as public USGS).
