Hypothesis backlog

Each card states which layers it uses, the physical signature it looks for, and — the part that matters — the specific reason it should catch a gap in the catalogue rather than a fault the catalogue already has. Phase 2 is scored by geologists reading exactly this reasoning, so the cards are written for that reader rather than for a model card.

Every card is untested. No spatial holdout has been run in this repository because the competition rasters are not placed in data/ (see pipeline status). “Untested” here means “not measured”, not “plausible but unexamined” — the reasoning is sourced, the numbers are not.

Cards

H1 — Newly mapped geometry of existing systems

untested

Layers
Training-label raster used only as a mask; 100 m detrended elevation (band 12) and slope (band 19); magnetic horizontal gradient (band 3) and tilt/curvature (band 6); 1 m lidar if placed.
Physical signature
A slope break or potential-field edge that continues along strike past the digitised end of a mapped trace, or runs parallel to it within a few hundred metres, or diverges from it as a splay.
Why a gap, not a known fault
Three independent supports. (1) Staff: “new fault” means “any fault pixel not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS” and “can include newly mapped geometry of an existing fault system” (11536). (2) Staff: a new-fault ground-truth pixel can lie within 300 m of a known trace, and “identifying these corrections is one outcome we are aiming for as part of this competition” (11516 post 4). (3) Hermant et al. measure catalogue-to-truth misfit of up to 400 m in north-central Nevada — larger than the 300 m kernel — which means some catalogue traces themselves sit off the scarp.
Expected DTI impact
Potentially the highest of any card, because it targets the class staff have confirmed exists in the label set. The risk is equally high and specific: the mask is pixel-exact, and a predicted pixel near a known trace but far from a new-fault label is fully penalised. Thickening range fronts indiscriminately is therefore not a hedge, it is pure cost.
Cost
Low to medium. Morphological extrapolation along strike plus a narrow-corridor model. No new data purchase; lidar improves it but is not required to start.
Validation protocol
Spatial holdout by contiguous block. Baseline: the mask itself (score zero by construction). Compare a corridor model against a control that adds the same probability mass at random offsets along strike. Score with scripts/metrics.py using the pixel-exact mask.
Rejection criterion
Reject if the added corridor is dominated by drainage ditches, irrigation lines, fan-channel margins or road embankments — i.e. if the same mass placed at random azimuths scores the same.

H2 — Buried intra-basin structures under cover

untested

Layers
Isostatic gravity anomaly (13) and its horizontal (18) and vertical (11) gradients and slope (5); magnetic RTP (2), TMI horizontal gradient (3), tilt/curvature (6), vertical gradient (9); depth to basement (15) as the stratifying variable. Not the label raster.
Physical signature
Linear gravity and/or magnetic gradient maxima — ideally in both families — beneath basins where depth-to-basement is large and surface slope is low. A density or susceptibility contrast with no surface scarp.
Why a gap, not a known fault
The USGS database is defined as faults with “geological evidence of coseismic surface deformation” in the past 1.6 Ma (USGS faults page), and the 1993 guidelines say structures without Quaternary movement “will not be shown unless there is compelling evidence” (OF 93-338). A buried structure with no surface rupture is not an oversight — it is out of scope by design. The organisers' own About page adds that in the GeoDAWN region “many are hidden below the surface, requiring geophysical data to detect”.
Expected DTI impact
High if the private labels include geophysically identified structures; harmful if they are lidar scarps only. Staff have declined to say which (11527 post 7), so this must be sized as a component, not as the whole submission. Phase 2 review can credit it even if Phase 1 DTI is weaker — the same staff post makes that point explicitly.
Cost
Low on the 100 m grids; high only if you reprocess GeoDAWN line data, which nothing here requires.
Validation protocol
Condition every candidate on band 15 and report detection statistics per cover-thickness class. Require conjunction of a gravity edge and a magnetic edge for high-confidence candidates. Cross-check against H7 before believing any of them.
Rejection criterion
Reject wherever candidates align with GeoDAWN survey or block boundaries (H7), or wherever a magnetic edge has no gravity counterpart over thin cover — the lithologic-contact explanation is then more parsimonious.

H3 — 1 m lidar scarps absent from the compiled map

blocked untested

Layers
1 m DEM from 1m_DEM_links.csv (officially provided, not external); 100 m detrended elevation (12) and slope (19) as coarse priors; the label raster as mask.
Physical signature
Linear breaks-in-slope and curvature maxima, 1–10 m of relief, persistent along strike for hundreds of metres to kilometres, on piedmonts and playa margins; frequently invisible at 100 m.
Why a gap, not a known fault
The 1993 guidelines told compilers to prefer “fault-related topical studies over general studies” and “more detailed scale of work” (OF 93-338), i.e. published work. A scarp that only became mappable after 3DEP lidar could not have been compiled from the literature at the time, no matter how clear it is now. Hermant et al. built an entirely new 1,100-fault manual label set for exactly this reason, stating the USGS mapping is “sometimes inaccurate at small scales or of variable precision”.
Expected DTI impact
High if the expert labels were made with lidar — likely, since the About page says 3DEP lidar was collected over a similar extent to the geophysical surveys — but unverified: staff will not confirm label provenance. Confusers are named and specific: ditches, shorelines, fan-channel margins, lithologic benches, fence and road lines, playa edges.
Cost
High. Tile download at 1 m, slope/curvature computation at native resolution, then aggregation to a 100 m probability. This is the most expensive card by an order of magnitude.
Blocker
Needs the data tab CSV and the rasters. Additionally, Hermant et al. report that clean slope is only obtainable where the DEM is lidar-derived — non-lidar 3DEP slope was artefact-ridden in their study. Build a lidar-coverage mask before computing anything.
Rejection criterion
Reject if candidate lineaments correlate better with the drainage and road network than with the fault catalogue's orientation distribution.

H4 — Kinematic orphans

untested

Layers
Geodetic second invariant (4), shear rate (7), dilatation rate (8); earthquake density (16) and distance-to-earthquake (10); label raster as mask.
Physical signature
Elevated strain-rate invariant and/or earthquake density whose nearest catalogue pixel is more than 300 m away.
Why a gap, not a known fault
Geodetic strain and seismicity are measured independently of whether anyone has published a scarp. INGENIOUS compiled them as separate evidence layers (GDR 1391), and the relationship between strain rate and earthquake rate in the western US is itself a research question (Kreemer & Young, 2022). A kinematic orphan is a location where the crust is demonstrably deforming and the surface catalogue says nothing.
Expected DTI impact
Low as a standalone map — these are smooth regional fields and will spray false positives. α=0.2 makes false positives cheap but not free. Use as a spatial prior that re-weights H1–H3 candidates.
Cost
Low. Everything is already in the 100 m stack. Note that bands 10 and 16 share parameters (n=100 km, a=15°) and are therefore not independent — use one.
Validation protocol
Quantile-bin bands 4, 7, 8, 10, 16; compute distance-to-nearest-label per bin on a spatial holdout. Monotonic decrease is the test.
Rejection criterion
Reject as a standalone detector if distance-to-label does not decrease with strain quantile. As a prior, reject only if it fails to improve the precision of H1–H3 candidates at matched recall.

H5 — Do not train to reproduce the raster the scorer removes

untested

Layers
All 19 bands. Labels used only as the scoring mask and as a near-trace hard-negative sampler — never as the positive class.
Physical signature
Not applicable. This is a training-design hypothesis.
Why a gap, not a known fault
The official reference solution trains a U-Net to reproduce the USGS/INGENIOUS raster (reference solution; see PA-3). Staff have confirmed the scoring mask is “identical to the provided set of training fault labels” (11516 post 4), so a perfect reproduction of the training target scores zero. Hermant et al. reached the same conclusion independently and built their own labels instead.
Expected DTI impact
A necessary condition for any deep-learning approach to beat “copy the catalogue”. Magnitude unknown; it is a precondition, not a gain.
Cost
Medium — retraining. GPU required for a serious run; the reference solution ships CPU and Apple-Silicon environments too.
Validation protocol
Train two models identical except for the positive-class definition; evaluate both on held-out geographic blocks with the pixel-exact mask. Also switch from the reference's random patch splits to contiguous spatial blocks, since random patches share edges and artefacts and will overstate generalisation.
Rejection criterion
Reject the reformulation only if it scores no better than the catalogue-reproduction baseline on a spatial holdout — in which case the model is learning nothing beyond the ink, and the effort belongs in H1–H3 instead.

H6 — Conductivity as corroboration, not detection

untested

Layers
Conductivity surface (band 17); optionally the five depth-sliced conductance maps from Peacock & Bedrosian (2022) if licensed for sharing.
Physical signature
A conductive lineament, or a shallow conductive base, coincident with an H1–H4 candidate.
Why a gap, not a known fault
Fluids, clays and alteration along an unmapped structure can raise conductance with no QFFD entry. But conductance is equally sensitive to basin fill, salinity and geothermal outflow — none of which is a fault — so it corroborates rather than discovers.
Expected DTI impact
Small and positive as a multiplier on structurally derived candidates. Expected negative if used as a primary map: it will light up basin centres, which is exactly where Hermant et al. warn that predictions should be treated with caution.
Cost
Low on the provided band. The depth-sliced external release is the improvement worth having — a conductor at 2 km means something different from one at 50 km, and the single stacked band cannot distinguish them.
Validation protocol
Measure candidate precision at matched recall with and without the conductivity multiplier, on a spatial holdout.
Rejection criterion
Reject as a primary detector. Reject as a multiplier if it does not improve precision at matched recall.

H7 — Control: the GeoDAWN flight-spec mosaic

untested

Layers
Magnetic gradients (bands 3, 6, 9) and gravity gradients (18); the GeoDAWN survey-outline and flight-path shapefiles shipped with the GeoDAWN release.
Physical signature
A step change in apparent lineament density or gradient amplitude at the Area 1 / Area 2 boundary, or lineaments paralleling acquisition-block boundaries, with no counterpart in gravity, DEM or the catalogue.
Why this is a control, not a gap
Area 1 was flown at 200 m line spacing and Area 2 at 400 m, at different terrain clearances, across four acquisition blocks flown by different contractors and aircraft (USGS GeoDAWN page). Any of those boundaries can manufacture a straight, fault-shaped feature. The USGS page also warns that in steep terrain “variable terrain clearance should be considered when modeling and interpreting these data”.
Expected DTI impact
Prevents false positives. No true positives expected — this card's value is entirely in what it removes from H2.
Cost
Low, and it is the only card that can be started without the competition rasters: the survey-outline shapefiles are public and CC0.
Validation protocol
Overlay the outlines on the gradient bands; test whether band 3/6 maxima cluster along boundary geometry more than expected under a random-orientation null.
Rejection criterion
If no boundary association is found, keep the card as a passing control and stop spending on it. A clean result is a result.

H8 — Inferred and blank-scale traces mark the edge of the map, not a positive class

untested

Layers
INGENIOUS 2023-06-27 attributes FTYPE_ and MAPSCALE from the NBMG service. Once rasters exist, the label raster is a mask only. This card is not a detector.
Physical signature
Not a geophysical signature. A catalogue-process signature: traces the compiler coded Inferred (5,280 of 22,956), Poor (100), Other (27), blank FTYPE_ (447), or blank MAPSCALE (4,059). Counts and query links: requirements checklist.
Why a gap, not a known fault
These traces are already in the compilation rules §3.3 says the training labels came from. Predicting them scores nothing if they are in the pixel-exact mask, and predicting next to them is fully penalised when no new-fault label is there (11516 post 4). The gap is the opposite of the ink: where an Inferred trace ends, and where the survey has no trace at all. The 22,956 count is the regional service, not the GeoDAWN raster. No clip has been run, so it is not yet known whether the Inferred population even sits in the scored area.
Expected DTI impact
Harmful if used as a positive class. Useful only as a spatial prior for H1, and only after the GeoDAWN clip shows a non-trivial Inferred population inside the survey.
Cost
Low. The attribute query is public. The clip needs the ScienceBase outline shapefiles, which this session could not download (TLS).
Validation protocol
Clip trace length by FTYPE_ to the official GeoDAWN outlines. Reject the prior if almost all Inferred length lies outside the survey. If it does not, measure distance from Inferred endpoints to the nearest Well Constrained trace, inside the outline only.
Rejection criterion
Reject as a detector immediately. Reject as a prior if the GeoDAWN clip shows the Inferred class is negligible inside the survey, or if endpoint distance does not separate holdout new-fault labels from random azimuths.

Negative results

None yet — because nothing has been run. This section exists so that the first negative result has somewhere to live. When a hypothesis is rejected it stays on this page with its rejection reason and the date, and the card is marked rejected. Rejected hypotheses are not deleted: “we tried this and it did not work, here is the measurement” is evidence, and it is exactly the kind of evidence a Phase 2 reviewer respects.

Recommended order once data land

  1. H8's clip, then H7 — both need only public shapefiles. H8 asks where the catalogue says it is least constrained. H7 asks whether survey boundaries fake structure. Neither could be downloaded from this machine on 2026-09-26 (TLS). On a machine that can, do these before any magnetic model.
  2. H5's split fix — move to contiguous spatial blocks before believing any score. Cheap, and it changes the meaning of every other number.
  3. H4 measurement — a single quantile-versus-distance table. Half a day, and it either kills or promotes the cheapest prior in the stack.
  4. H2 with band-15 conditioning — the highest-upside geophysical card, and conditioning costs nothing.
  5. H1 — highest expected value, and the one staff have effectively endorsed; but only after the mask behaviour is confirmed locally.
  6. H3 last — highest cost, and gated on lidar-coverage mapping.
