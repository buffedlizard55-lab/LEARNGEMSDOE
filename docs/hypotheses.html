Hypothesis backlog

Each card states which layers it uses, the physical signature it looks for, and — the part that matters — the specific reason it should catch a gap in the catalogue rather than a fault the catalogue already has. Phase 2 is scored by geologists reading exactly this reasoning, so the cards are written for that reader rather than for a model card.

Every card except H15 is untested against any label set. H15 is measured on synthetic tiles only (geometry under the metric, not labels). For all other cards, no spatial holdout has been run in this repository because the competition rasters are not placed in data/ (see pipeline status). “Untested” here means “not measured”, not “plausible but unexamined” — the reasoning is sourced, the numbers are not. Two cards are additionally blocked on their own inputs: H3 (the 1 m DEM links as well as the rasters) and H10 (an external lidar fault mapping, independent of the competition data).

Cards

H1 — Newly mapped geometry of existing systems

untested

Layers
Training-label raster used only as a mask; 100 m detrended elevation (band 12) and slope (band 19); magnetic horizontal gradient (band 3) and tilt/curvature (band 6); 1 m lidar if placed.
Physical signature
A slope break or potential-field edge that continues along strike past the digitised end of a mapped trace, or runs parallel to it within a few hundred metres, or diverges from it as a splay.
Why a gap, not a known fault
Three independent supports. (1) Staff: “new fault” means “any fault pixel not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS” and “can include newly mapped geometry of an existing fault system” (11536). (2) Staff: a new-fault ground-truth pixel can lie within 300 m of a known trace, and “identifying these corrections is one outcome we are aiming for as part of this competition” (11516 post 4). (3) Hermant et al. measure catalogue-to-truth misfit of up to 400 m in north-central Nevada — larger than the 300 m kernel — which means some catalogue traces themselves sit off the scarp.
Expected DTI impact
Potentially the highest of any card, because it targets the class staff have confirmed exists in the label set. The risk is equally high and specific: the mask is pixel-exact, and a predicted pixel near a known trace but far from a new-fault label is fully penalised. Thickening range fronts indiscriminately is therefore not a hedge, it is pure cost.
Cost
Low to medium. Morphological extrapolation along strike plus a narrow-corridor model. No new data purchase; lidar improves it but is not required to start.
Validation protocol
Spatial holdout by contiguous block. Baseline: the mask itself (score zero by construction). Compare a corridor model against a control that adds the same probability mass at random offsets along strike. Score with scripts/metrics.py using the pixel-exact mask.
Rejection criterion
Reject if the added corridor is dominated by drainage ditches, irrigation lines, fan-channel margins or road embankments — i.e. if the same mass placed at random azimuths scores the same.

H2 — Buried intra-basin structures under cover

untested

Layers
Isostatic gravity anomaly (13) and its horizontal (18) and vertical (11) gradients and slope (5); magnetic RTP (2), TMI horizontal gradient (3), tilt/curvature (6), vertical gradient (9); depth to basement (15) as the stratifying variable. Not the label raster.
Physical signature
Linear gravity and/or magnetic gradient maxima — ideally in both families — beneath basins where depth-to-basement is large and surface slope is low. A density or susceptibility contrast with no surface scarp.
Why a gap, not a known fault
The USGS database is defined as faults with “geological evidence of coseismic surface deformation” in the past 1.6 Ma (USGS faults page), and the 1993 guidelines say structures without Quaternary movement “will not be shown unless there is compelling evidence” (OF 93-338). A buried structure with no surface rupture is not an oversight — it is out of scope by design. The organisers' own About page adds that in the GeoDAWN region “many are hidden below the surface, requiring geophysical data to detect”.
Expected DTI impact
High if the private labels include geophysically identified structures; harmful if they are lidar scarps only. Staff have declined to say which (11527 post 7), so this must be sized as a component, not as the whole submission. Phase 2 review can credit it even if Phase 1 DTI is weaker — the same staff post makes that point explicitly.
Cost
Low on the 100 m grids; high only if you reprocess GeoDAWN line data, which nothing here requires.
Validation protocol
Condition every candidate on band 15 and report detection statistics per cover-thickness class. Require conjunction of a gravity edge and a magnetic edge for high-confidence candidates. Cross-check against H7 before believing any of them.
Rejection criterion
Reject wherever candidates align with GeoDAWN survey or block boundaries (H7), or wherever a magnetic edge has no gravity counterpart over thin cover — the lithologic-contact explanation is then more parsimonious.

H3 — 1 m lidar scarps absent from the compiled map

blocked untested

Layers
1 m DEM from 1m_DEM_links.csv (officially provided, not external); 100 m detrended elevation (12) and slope (19) as coarse priors; the label raster as mask.
Physical signature
Linear breaks-in-slope and curvature maxima, 1–10 m of relief, persistent along strike for hundreds of metres to kilometres, on piedmonts and playa margins; frequently invisible at 100 m.
Why a gap, not a known fault
The 1993 guidelines told compilers to prefer “fault-related topical studies over general studies” and “more detailed scale of work” (OF 93-338), i.e. published work. A scarp that only became mappable after 3DEP lidar could not have been compiled from the literature at the time, no matter how clear it is now. Hermant et al. built an entirely new 1,100-fault manual label set for exactly this reason, stating the USGS mapping is “sometimes inaccurate at small scales or of variable precision”.
Expected DTI impact
High if the expert labels were made with lidar — likely, since the About page says 3DEP lidar was collected over a similar extent to the geophysical surveys — but unverified: staff will not confirm label provenance. Confusers are named and specific: ditches, shorelines, fan-channel margins, lithologic benches, fence and road lines, playa edges.
Cost
High. Tile download at 1 m, slope/curvature computation at native resolution, then aggregation to a 100 m probability. This is the most expensive card by an order of magnitude.
Blocker
Needs the data tab CSV and the rasters. Additionally, Hermant et al. report that clean slope is only obtainable where the DEM is lidar-derived — non-lidar 3DEP slope was artefact-ridden in their study. Build a lidar-coverage mask before computing anything.
Rejection criterion
Reject if candidate lineaments correlate better with the drainage and road network than with the fault catalogue's orientation distribution.

H4 — Kinematic orphans

untested

Layers
Geodetic second invariant (4), shear rate (7), dilatation rate (8); earthquake density (16) and distance-to-earthquake (10); label raster as mask.
Physical signature
Elevated strain-rate invariant and/or earthquake density whose nearest catalogue pixel is more than 300 m away.
Why a gap, not a known fault
Geodetic strain and seismicity are measured independently of whether anyone has published a scarp. INGENIOUS compiled them as separate evidence layers (GDR 1391), and the relationship between strain rate and earthquake rate in the western US is itself a research question (Kreemer & Young, 2022). A kinematic orphan is a location where the crust is demonstrably deforming and the surface catalogue says nothing.
Expected DTI impact
Low as a standalone map — these are smooth regional fields and will spray false positives. α=0.2 makes false positives cheap but not free. Use as a spatial prior that re-weights H1–H3 candidates.
Cost
Low. Everything is already in the 100 m stack. Note that bands 10 and 16 share parameters (n=100 km, a=15°) and are therefore not independent — use one.
Validation protocol
Quantile-bin bands 4, 7, 8, 10, 16; compute distance-to-nearest-label per bin on a spatial holdout. Monotonic decrease is the test.
Rejection criterion
Reject as a standalone detector if distance-to-label does not decrease with strain quantile. As a prior, reject only if it fails to improve the precision of H1–H3 candidates at matched recall.

H5 — Do not train to reproduce the raster the scorer removes

untested

Layers
All 19 bands. Labels used only as the scoring mask and as a near-trace hard-negative sampler — never as the positive class.
Physical signature
Not applicable. This is a training-design hypothesis.
Why a gap, not a known fault
The official reference solution trains a U-Net to reproduce the USGS/INGENIOUS raster (reference solution; see PA-3). Staff have confirmed the scoring mask is “identical to the provided set of training fault labels” (11516 post 4), so a perfect reproduction of the training target scores zero. Hermant et al. reached the same conclusion independently and built their own labels instead.
Expected DTI impact
A necessary condition for any deep-learning approach to beat “copy the catalogue”. Magnitude unknown; it is a precondition, not a gain.
Cost
Medium — retraining. GPU required for a serious run; the reference solution ships CPU and Apple-Silicon environments too.
Validation protocol
Train two models identical except for the positive-class definition; evaluate both on held-out geographic blocks with the pixel-exact mask. Also switch from the reference's random patch splits to contiguous spatial blocks, since random patches share edges and artefacts and will overstate generalisation.
Rejection criterion
Reject the reformulation only if it scores no better than the catalogue-reproduction baseline on a spatial holdout — in which case the model is learning nothing beyond the ink, and the effort belongs in H1–H3 instead.

H6 — Conductivity as corroboration, not detection

untested

Layers
Conductivity surface (band 17); optionally the five depth-sliced conductance maps from Peacock & Bedrosian (2022) if licensed for sharing.
Physical signature
A conductive lineament, or a shallow conductive base, coincident with an H1–H4 candidate.
Why a gap, not a known fault
Fluids, clays and alteration along an unmapped structure can raise conductance with no QFFD entry. But conductance is equally sensitive to basin fill, salinity and geothermal outflow — none of which is a fault — so it corroborates rather than discovers.
Expected DTI impact
Small and positive as a multiplier on structurally derived candidates. Expected negative if used as a primary map: it will light up basin centres, which is exactly where Hermant et al. warn that predictions should be treated with caution.
Cost
Low on the provided band. The depth-sliced external release is the improvement worth having — a conductor at 2 km means something different from one at 50 km, and the single stacked band cannot distinguish them.
Validation protocol
Measure candidate precision at matched recall with and without the conductivity multiplier, on a spatial holdout.
Rejection criterion
Reject as a primary detector. Reject as a multiplier if it does not improve precision at matched recall.

H7 — Control: the GeoDAWN flight-spec mosaic

untested

Layers
Magnetic gradients (bands 3, 6, 9) and gravity gradients (18); the GeoDAWN survey-outline and flight-path shapefiles shipped with the GeoDAWN release.
Physical signature
A step change in apparent lineament density or gradient amplitude at the Area 1 / Area 2 boundary, or lineaments paralleling acquisition-block boundaries, with no counterpart in gravity, DEM or the catalogue.
Why this is a control, not a gap
Area 1 was flown at 200 m line spacing and Area 2 at 400 m, at different terrain clearances, across four acquisition blocks flown by different contractors and aircraft (USGS GeoDAWN page). Any of those boundaries can manufacture a straight, fault-shaped feature. The USGS page also warns that in steep terrain “variable terrain clearance should be considered when modeling and interpreting these data”.
Expected DTI impact
Prevents false positives. No true positives expected — this card's value is entirely in what it removes from H2.
Cost
Low, and it is the only card that can be started without the competition rasters: the survey-outline shapefiles are public and CC0.
Validation protocol
Overlay the outlines on the gradient bands; test whether band 3/6 maxima cluster along boundary geometry more than expected under a random-orientation null.
Rejection criterion
If no boundary association is found, keep the card as a passing control and stop spending on it. A clean result is a result.

H8 — Inferred and blank-scale traces mark the edge of the map, not a positive class

rejected 2026-09-26 footprint census measured

Rejected by its own pre-stated criterion. The footprint clip (CG-12) finds Inferred = 8 of 413 traces, 51.0 km of 6,230.2 km (0.8%), and zero Poor/Other/blank. The poorly-constrained stock this card relied on is not in the GeoDAWN area. Superseded by H12. Original reasoning kept below.

Layers
INGENIOUS 2023-06-27 attributes FTYPE_ and MAPSCALE from the NBMG service. Once rasters exist, the label raster is a mask only. This card is not a detector.
Physical signature
Not a geophysical signature. A catalogue-process signature: traces the compiler coded Inferred (5,280 of 22,956), Poor (100), Other (27), blank FTYPE_ (447), or blank MAPSCALE (4,059). Counts and query links: requirements checklist.
Why a gap, not a known fault
These traces are already in the compilation rules §3.3 says the training labels came from. Predicting them scores nothing if they are in the pixel-exact mask, and predicting next to them is fully penalised when no new-fault label is there (11516 post 4). The gap is the opposite of the ink: where an Inferred trace ends, and where the survey has no trace at all. The 22,956 count is the regional service, not the GeoDAWN raster.
What the census changed (2026-09-26)
The envelope clip is now measured — CG-9: inside the GeoDAWN bounding box the compilation holds 1,179 traces — Well Constrained 739, Moderately Constrained 351, Inferred 89, and zero Poor / Other / blank; blank MAPSCALE also zero. The polygon clip is still missing, so these remain upper bounds on the footprint. Update 2026-09-26 (session dfd3): polygon clip done — 413 traces, Inferred 8 (CG-12).

inference Inside the box, Inferred is 7.5% of traces against 23.0% regionally. If that survives the polygon clip, the GeoDAWN area is better mapped than the region as a whole, and this card's value shifts: less "a stock of poorly constrained traces to extend", more "the well-constrained traces here are the ones to trust positionally — concentrate on geometry they do not contain at all", i.e. CG-7 classes 2–3 over class 4.

Expected DTI impact
Harmful if used as a positive class. Useful only as a spatial prior for H1, and only after the GeoDAWN polygon clip shows a non-trivial Inferred population inside the survey.
Cost
Low. The attribute queries are public and linked from CG-9. The polygon clip needs the ScienceBase outline shapefiles, which this machine still cannot download (TLS).
Validation protocol
Clip trace length by FTYPE_ to the official GeoDAWN outlines. Reject the prior if almost all Inferred length lies outside the survey. If it does not, measure distance from Inferred endpoints to the nearest Well Constrained trace, inside the outline only.
Rejection criterion
Reject as a detector immediately. Reject as a prior if the GeoDAWN clip shows the Inferred class is negligible inside the survey, or if endpoint distance does not separate holdout new-fault labels from random azimuths.

H9 — Train against label incompleteness, not only label position

untested design hypothesis

Layers
All 19 bands; the label raster used as the scoring mask and as a noisy positive set. No external data.
Physical signature
None. This is a training-target hypothesis and it is written as one — it should not be dressed up as a geophysical claim.
Why a gap, not a known fault
The QFFD is defined by demonstrable coseismic surface deformation in the past 1.6 Ma (CG-1); its compilers were told to prefer published, recent, detailed-scale studies (CG-2); and Hermant et al. report model-found faults in this region that experts then confirmed on lidar (GM-7). The label raster is therefore a biased, incomplete sample of the fault population, not the population. A model trained to reproduce it learns the catalogue's accessibility and publication biases — the inverse of the scored target. Hermant et al. propose precisely the countermeasures proposed here: "inform the model that any part of the study area may contain some faults, even if they are not present in the fault label", via an adversarial loss term or label noise (PA-7).
Expected DTI impact
Not measurable alone; it is the mechanism that lets H1–H3 candidates carry probability mass outside catalogue corridors. The asymmetry helps: α = 0.2 prices a false positive at a quarter of a false negative, so the error this introduces is the cheap kind. The risk is specific: label noise could degrade the near-known-trace corrections that staff describe as "one outcome we are aiming for" (11516 post 4) — the most valuable pixels in the submission.
Cost
Medium — one retrain, and a hyperparameter (noise rate or adversarial weight) that cannot be read off the data statistics.
Validation protocol
Two models identical except for the incompleteness term, scored with scripts/metrics.py (α = 0.2, β = 0.8, R = 300 m, pixel-exact mask) on contiguous geographic blocks held out. With no private labels locally, measure three proxies: (1) the share of predicted mass outside the 300 m corridor of known traces; (2) a hand-audit of 50 random high-probability non-catalogue pixels against 1 m lidar and imagery; (3) recall on deliberately withheld catalogue traces, as a check that the noise has not destroyed the ability to find faults at all.
Rejection criterion
Reject if the added out-of-corridor mass is dominated by survey or block-boundary geometry (H7) or by GM-8's confuser list. Reject specifically as a noise method if withheld-trace recall falls faster than out-of-corridor precision rises.

H10 — An independent lidar validation anchor at Rye Patch / western Humboldt Range

blocked untested

Layers
Silver et al. (2011) lidar-derived fault mapping (doi:10.1130/GES00673.1); bands 12 and 19; the officially provided 1 m DEM; the label raster as a mask only.
Physical signature
Faults mapped from lidar in that study that are absent from, or displaced relative to, the USGS catalogue.
Why a gap, not a known fault
It is an independent expert lidar mapping that predates this competition, inside the GeoDAWN bounding box — Hermant et al.'s Figure 9 prints 40°36′ N with longitudes 117°36′–117°42′ W, Figure 8 prints 40°30′–40°33′ N, and both sit inside the box recorded in CG-9. Longitudes for Figure 8 are not printed in the figure, so no longitude is claimed for the Rye Patch panel. Running a candidate detector there and asking "did it recover faults the catalogue lacks?" is a genuine out-of-sample test of gap-finding behaviour that costs no submission slot and touches no private label.
Expected DTI impact
None directly. Its job is to de-risk H1 and H3 with a local truth set before any scoring budget is spent.
Cost
Low to medium, entirely gated on whether the mapping is distributed.
Validation protocol
Treat Silver et al.'s lidar faults as truth; score detectors with scripts/metrics.py under two masks — the catalogue alone (what we have) and the catalogue plus Silver (what a better catalogue looks like). A detector whose score improves sharply under the second mask is finding real un-catalogued faults, not artefacts.
Rejection criterion
Reject as an anchor if the mapping is not obtainable, or if its footprint falls outside the actual GeoDAWN flight lines rather than merely outside the bounding box.
Open risk
Neither the paper nor any accompanying data has been obtained. It is a lead (PA-8), not an asset.

H11 — Soft confuser channels instead of hard negative classes

untested

Layers
Drainage / channel network and pluvial-shoreline elevations as continuous channels (external, public); bands 12 and 19 for the candidate set; the label raster as a mask only.
Physical signature
A candidate lineament that coincides with a channel margin or with a paleo-shoreline elevation contour.
Why a gap, not a known fault
Hermant et al. measured paleo-shorelines, canyon boundaries and stream boundaries being detected as faults in this terrain — and warned that a dedicated confuser class "may therefore miss some of these co-located structures", because "faults can be co-located with a paleo-shoreline … or with a canyon boundary" (GM-8). A soft channel suppresses the confuser without deleting the fault that happens to run along it, which is the case a geologist would most want kept.
Expected DTI impact
Small and positive — precision at matched recall, with the false-positive term priced at α = 0.2. Not a headline gain; a way to spend less of the audit budget on ditches.
Cost
Low to medium. The layers are public; the engineering is a channel concatenation.
Validation protocol
Precision at matched recall on a contiguous-block holdout, with and without the channels — plus an explicit check that candidates co-located with mapped faults are not suppressed, which is the failure mode Hermant et al. warn about.
Rejection criterion
Reject if precision at matched recall does not improve, or if it removes H1 near-trace correction candidates — the most valuable pixels in the whole submission.

H12 — Coarse-scale compilation: the gap is below 1:250k resolution and inside the positional error

untested motivating count verified (CG-12) mechanism: inference

Layers
Label raster (mask only); 1 m DEM tiles via 1m_DEM_links.csv; 100 m detrended elevation and slope (bands 12, 19); INGENIOUS v2 MAPSCALE / FTYPE_ as metadata.
Physical signature
(a) Short, low-relief scarps in the 1 m DEM with no catalogue trace; (b) a 1 m scarp sub-parallel to a catalogue trace but offset from it by more than the 300 m kernel.
Why a gap, not a known fault
All 413 footprint traces carry MAPSCALE 250 or 100; none carries the regionally dominant 10 or 24 (CG-12, verified). The v2 field-definition README defines these as 1:250,000 and 1:100,000 and says such faults "could be more discontinuous than continuous" (verified; rejection condition 1 cleared 2026-09-26). At 1:250k, 1 mm on the source map is 250 m on the ground, so generalisation error alone is of the order of the 300 m kernel (arithmetic, not a measured error). Hermant et al. measured catalogue misfit up to 400 m in north-central Nevada (H1). Both classes are pixels the mask does not contain — staff's definition of "new" (11536).
Expected DTI impact
Potentially large for (b), riding on H1's staff-confirmed class; moderate for (a). Risk: the pixel-exact mask fully penalises near-trace predictions that are not on a new label.
Cost
Medium — 1 m DEM tiles (URLs in the login-gated competition CSV) plus a scarp detector. Correction recorded 2026-09-26 (CG-15, CG-16, H13). The scale arithmetic over-predicts positional error: the two public compilations agree to ≤31.6 m inside the footprint, an order of magnitude inside the kernel. Class (b) therefore cannot be sized from compilation-to-compilation offsets — it must come from expert re-mapping at higher resolution (H10, GM-7's >150 m, CG-5's 400 m). The observation that the footprint is a coarse-scale compilation is now corroborated twice: every INGENIOUS footprint trace is MAPSCALE 250 or 100 (CG-12), and 87.8% of the 5,570 USGS footprint sections carry scale = 1:250,000 (CG-16).
Validation protocol
Contiguous-block spatial holdout; distance from high-curvature 1 m scarps to nearest catalogue trace, stratified by MAPSCALE. Prediction: a heavier >300 m tail for code-250 traces than for code-100 traces.
Rejection criterion
Reject if the v2 field definitions show MAPSCALE is not a scale denominator, or if the distance distribution shows no >300 m tail versus a random-offset control.

H13 — Compilation-to-compilation offsets as a proxy for the correction class

rejected as a proxy 2026-09-26 measured

Layers
Public USGS QFFD GIS distribution (Qfaults_GIS.zip), the public INGENIOUS Qfaults v2 compilation, and the public GeoDAWN data-extent outline. No competition raster, no submission slot.
Physical signature
None — this is a measurement about the catalogues, not about the crust.
Why it was worth testing
Staff define a “new fault” to include corrections to existing traces and say identifying those “is one outcome we are aiming for” (GV-15), and that such corrections may also be in the Final-Round evaluation set. Hermant et al. measure up to 400 m between USGS traces and their own lidar-derived labels (CG-5) and >150 m at Leach Hot Springs (GM-7). If two independent compilations of the same faults disagreed by hundreds of metres, the width of the corridor H1 must search would be readable straight off public data — the cheapest possible answer to the most expensive question in the backlog.
What was measured
Inside the GeoDAWN data extent, the USGS compilation holds 5,570 sections and 6,241.3 km of trace; INGENIOUS holds 413 traces and 6,230.2 km. Every one of the 413 INGENIOUS traces intersects a USGS section (0.0 m), and every one of the 5,570 USGS sections lies within 31.6 m of an INGENIOUS trace. Length difference 0.18%. (CG-15, run 36277952393.)
Why it is rejected
The two compilations agree to well inside the 300 m kernel. Positional disagreement between compilations is therefore not the mechanism that produces the correction class; expert re-mapping from higher-resolution data is. The corridor width cannot be read off the public catalogues and must come from H10 (Silver et al. lidar mapping) or from Hermant's measured discrepancies.
Cost of the test
One GitHub Actions run on public files. No competition data, no prediction, no submission slot.
What the test changed anyway
  1. It corrected this knowledge base's own claim. CG-12's “413 traces inside the data extent” is one compilation's record count, not the catalogue's content; the content is ~6,230 km of fault (CG-15). Several pages had read the count as content.
  2. It weakened H12's mechanism — H12's “1 mm at 1:250,000 = 250 m” arithmetic over-predicts by an order of magnitude. H12 class (a) is unaffected.
  3. It corroborated H12's observation on the USGS side: 87.8% of the 5,570 footprint sections carry scale = 1:250,000 (CG-16).
Rejection criterion (pre-stated, met)
Reject if the two compilations agree to well within the 300 m kernel. They agree to 31.6 m.

H14 — The catalogue's own recency attributes mark where expert attention has already been spent

untested premise measured

Layers
Label raster as mask; the catalogue's own recency attribute — USGS age, INGENIOUS REC2023 / RCODE2023 — as a spatial prior; bands 12, 19 and 3, 6 for the candidate set.
Physical signature
None. This is a prior about where geologists have already looked, derived from the catalogue's own metadata rather than from any geophysical layer.
Why it should catch a gap rather than a known fault
Measured inside the GeoDAWN footprint (CG-16): of 5,570 USGS sections, 715 (12.8%) are historic, 1,928 (34.6%) latest Quaternary, 2,335 (41.9%) only “undifferentiated Quaternary”, 562 late Quaternary and 30 middle-to-late Quaternary. Nearly half the catalogue in the survey area records Holocene rupture — that is the material field campaigns, lidar passes and expert mapping have already been spent on (GM-3, GM-7). Where the catalogue can only say “undifferentiated Quaternary”, nobody has done the work that would resolve it, and CG-13's publication gate means that unresolved work is exactly the work most likely to be missing. So unmapped fault should be relatively more likely in the undifferentiated-Quaternary parts of the footprint, not less.
Expected DTI impact
Low as a standalone prior; potentially useful as a re-weighting of H1–H3 candidates. It is cheap — the attribute is already in the public shapefile, and the label raster is masked anyway.
Cost
Low.
Validation protocol
  1. No competition data needed. Test the prior against the catalogues themselves: are INGENIOUS's updated traces (the ones that differ from the USGS compilation) concentrated in the parts of the footprint where the USGS compilation says “undifferentiated Quaternary”? That is a direct, public-data test of whether recency metadata predicts where re-mapping happened.
  2. Once rasters are placed: bin candidate pixels by the recency class of the nearest catalogue trace and measure label density per bin.
Rejection criterion
Reject if new-label density per unit area shows no variation with recency class, or if route (1) shows no association between INGENIOUS's updates and the USGS's undifferentiated-Quaternary sections.
Confidence
verified for the recency counts (CG-16). inference for the reasoning that they predict expert attention; route (1) exists precisely to test it.

H15 — Metric-aware node placement: when spaced emission beats dense, and where it fails

partially validated 2026-09-26 · synthetic tiles measured with our metric transfer to real traces: inference

Layers
None — this is an emission-policy result measured on synthetic traces with this repository's own distance-weighted Tversky implementation (scripts/placement_check.py driving scripts/metrics.py). No competition data consumed; nothing emitted that could be submitted.
The claim under test
The metric credits each truth pixel from its single best prediction within a 300 m triangular kernel and charges every emitted pixel that covers nothing, so at a fixed pixel budget, spaced nodes along a candidate trace should beat a dense ridge prefix whenever there is more trace than budget. The sibling Pindrop page (site claim, GV-20) publishes square-suppressed nodes at spacing k = 4, derived from spacing/2 < R ⇒ spacing ≤ 5.
What was measured (truth 72 px, budget 40 px, hard emission on the trace)
  • Axis-aligned trace: dense DTI 0.6231 vs square-suppressed s=4 0.7058 — the published advantage reproduces.
  • Oblique 1:2 (26.6°): 0.6202 vs 0.6570 — the advantage holds but shrinks.
  • 45° diagonal: 0.6176 vs 0.5764 — below the dense control. Square (Chebyshev) suppression forces diagonal nodes 5.66 px apart; the midpoint sits 2.83 px from the nearest node and earns kernel credit 0.0572.
  • The fix: disc (Euclidean) suppression at the same spacing scores 0.7257 on the diagonal — above the axis-aligned win — because it spends more nodes on diagonal segments, matching the isotropic kernel.
  • Regime condition: at budget = trace length, dense scores 1.000 vs s=4 nodes 0.6989 — the placement advantage exists only when coverage is the binding constraint. The competition regime is exactly that (hidden truth sparse vs any pixel budget), but the condition must be stated each time the policy is invoked.
  • Worst-case kernel credits: axis s=4 → 0.3333 (the published bound, true only for 4-connected geometry); diagonal square s=4 → 0.0572; s=6 axis → 0.0000 (a true coverage hole).
Expected DTI impact
Policy-level, a few hundredths of DTI either way at fixed detections; it changes no tier by itself (GV-19: the tier gap is a detection problem).
Cost
Trivial — one suppression functor.
Validation protocol
Once a single canonical entry is settled (GV-18): split any real emission by local trace orientation and score per segment. Prediction under the published square rule: segment DTI rises with |orientation − axis|.
Rejection criterion
If the orientation effect does not reproduce on real geometry, the synthetic model is over-simplified; this card then moves to Negative results with its numbers.
Confidence
measured on synthetic tiles (reproduce: python3 scripts/placement_check.py). Transfer to real traces is inference; the sibling pages' implementation details are site claims.
